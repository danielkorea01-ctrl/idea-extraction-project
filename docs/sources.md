# Sources and Evidence Limits

Version 0.1 | Reviewed 17 October 2026 (Asia/Seoul)

This is a focused starting bibliography, not a systematic literature review. Only the linked abstracts and bibliographic records were inspected for v0.1. No full-paper review or independent replication is claimed. The sources were selected to check candidate components and distinguish a commonly conflated mechanism.

## S1 — Sentence representations

Nils Reimers and Iryna Gurevych (2019). *Sentence-BERT: Sentence Embeddings using Siamese BERT-Networks*. EMNLP 2019.

https://arxiv.org/abs/1908.10084v1

Supports F1: a published method for semantic sentence representations. Does not establish idea originality, commercial usefulness, or a provider's use of conversations.

## S2 — Topic clustering

Maarten Grootendorst (2022). *BERTopic: Neural topic modeling with a class-based TF-IDF procedure*. arXiv preprint.

https://arxiv.org/abs/2203.05794v1

Supports F2: a published approach to grouping and representing document topics. Does not validate attribution or distinguish novel technical ideas from common themes.

## S3 — Training-data extraction

Nicholas Carlini et al. (2021 revision; originally submitted 2020). *Extracting Training Data from Large Language Models*.

https://arxiv.org/abs/2012.07805v2

Supports D1: the authors report training-text recovery from GPT-2. This concerns a different mechanism from deliberate product-intelligence extraction. It does not demonstrate private-chat mining or product-team appropriation.

## Coverage limits and next review

No provider policy, internal process, legal instrument, or individual copying allegation has been evaluated in this release. A later provider-policy review should record product, account type, jurisdiction, policy effective date, retrieval date, relevant section, and the distinction between stated permission and verified practice.

Future evidence entries must identify the exact claim supported, the source's limitations, contradictory evidence, and whether the result was independently reproduced. Do not treat search snippets or model-generated answers as primary evidence.
